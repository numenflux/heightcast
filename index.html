What actually determines your height

  • Genetics — about 80%. Most of your adult height is set by the DNA you inherited. Twin and family studies put the heritability of height around 0.8. That's why parents' heights are the single biggest input here. (Silventoinen et al., twin studies; Yengo et al., Nature 2022.)
  • Nutrition & overall health during your growing years. Long-run, balanced nutrition is what lets you reach your genetic potential — not any single food or pill. Chronic under-nutrition or illness can lower final height.
  • Sleep during puberty. Most growth hormone is released during deep sleep, so consistently good sleep while you're still growing supports normal growth.
  • Growth plates close after puberty. Height comes from growth plates at the ends of your bones. Once they fuse — typically by around 14–16 in girls and 16–18 in boys — you've reached your adult height and it doesn't increase. (General pediatric endocrinology.)

Honest FAQ

Can I increase my adult height?

Truthfully: once your growth plates have closed, no — no stretch, supplement, or routine adds permanent height. What you can change is how tall you look: standing up straight, core and back strength, and good posture can add a centimetre or two of apparent height. That's appearance, not bone.

Do the pills and "grow taller" programs work?

No. There's no supplement, powder, or exercise program that increases adult height after puberty. We will never sell or recommend one. If a site promises inches, it's selling you something.

How accurate is this estimate?

The Khamis‑Roche method is accurate to within about ±5 cm (±2 in) for most people, roughly 9 times out of 10. It's more accurate the closer you are to your adult height. We show a range on purpose — a single number would be pretending.

What if I don't know a parent's height?

Parents' heights power two of the three methods, so a good guess is better than nothing — but the estimate gets shakier. Use your best estimate and treat the range as wider than it looks.

Why do you need my weight?

The Khamis‑Roche regression uses current weight as one of its inputs (it slightly adjusts the prediction). It never leaves your phone, and we don't judge it — it's just a variable in a 1994 equation.
